# 1200 vs 600 baud: offline audio-timecode results

**2026-10-26 — preferred profile: 1200 baud.** Both profiles carried complete
audio-only timecodes through the tested G.711 paths. 1200 baud fits the existing
two-events-per-second schedule; 600 baud offers more noise margin and requires
one full packet per second with this framing. Neither is integrated into the
live generator or phone app yet.

## Evidence

The held-out run used **128 packets per stream/condition**, a new noise seed
20261026, 27 positive channel conditions for each of three profiles
(1200/600 at one event/s, plus 1200 at two events/s), and 12 negative-control
streams: **93 result rows**. The decoder received PCM audio only and discovered
the baud rate itself. Full source/event identity, the exact 64-bit send timestamp
and signed source AV offset were compared against the separate transmit manifest
only after decoding. Fixtures cross the old 24-bit wrap, carry offsets up to
1.4 seconds, and include a 3.217-second delay and mid-stream joins.

All **25 non-extreme-noise conditions** decoded every complete packet in all
three profiles. These include actual FFmpeg G.711 A/µ-law at 8 kHz, 300–3400-Hz
filtering, reduced level, AGC, clipping, 44.1-kHz resampling, ±500 ppm, Opus at
16/32 kbit/s, 20/10/6-dB AWGN, synthetic echoes, 10/20/40-ms data erasures,
20-ms repeated PLC-like segments, multi-second delay, random join and the
combined noise/echo path.

For the preferred **1200 baud / two events per second**, each of the main
G.711 A/µ-law, phone-filter, Opus, AGC, echo and 40-ms-erasure rows was **128/128**.
Random joining leaves 121 complete packets; all 121 were recovered.

At **0 dB SNR** the limit becomes visible:

- 1200 baud / one event/s: **65/128** (50.8%).
- 600 baud / one event/s: **128/128**.
- 1200 baud / two events/s: **62/128** (48.4%).

At **−6 dB SNR**, neither profile decoded a packet (0/128 in each profile).
The common chirp detector found only 18 prefix candidates in each stream, so
this is a failure of the complete acquisition/demodulation chain, not a pure
measurement of FSK sensitivity. No thresholds were relaxed after this held-out run.

**Zero incorrect packets were accepted** across the comparison. Wrong-CRC,
legacy-chirp, noise-only and unrelated-tone controls produced zero accepted
timecodes. This finite test does not establish an extremely low false-acceptance
rate. In total, 9840 of 10353 complete expected positive-channel packets were
accepted; the 513 missing packets all belong to the explicit 0/−6-dB stress rows.
An overall percentage would obscure those deliberately different conditions.

The phone-filter path shifted the recovered chirp anchor by approximately
0.046 ms. These are **synthetic sample-timeline observations**, not physical
phone UTC or capture-accuracy claims. Statistics apply only to accepted packets;
failed packets must be considered alongside timing results. The packet's roughly
422-ms decode wait at 1200 baud did not get added to the arrival timestamp.

## Reproduction and provenance

See [README](README.md), [native probe](probe.cpp), [driver](run.py) and the
[full machine-readable results](results-2026-10-26.json). The source/binary/WAV
hashes and compiler/FFmpeg versions are recorded. The candidate was developed
with a separate 24-packet exploratory run, then frozen for the larger run.
The packaged reproduction wrapper also passed a two-packet smoke run after its
build/import setup was checked. A Python struct/zlib wire-vector check independently
verified field sizes, byte order, 64-bit timestamp and CRC. ASan/UBSan self-tests
and a 128-packet native phone/µ-law decode passed without diagnostics.

The receiver's CRC-reject counter counts demodulation hypotheses, not unique
transmitted packets. It must not be read as a packet-loss total.

## What this does not establish

Real calls, phone acoustic playback/capture, neural noise suppression, live
WebRTC/SRT transport and production generator/iPhone integration remain pending.
Opus file coding is not an end-to-end WebRTC test. Source WAV timestamps are
fixed synthetic reference values, so playing one later is not a wall-clock
latency test. The offset field is checked as packet data; no video/offset-sweep scheduler or
legacy AV-pairer change was tested. Full packets at 1200 baud leave roughly
63 ms between events at 2/s, so per-event offset changes require explicit
overlap handling during integration. Wire-format fields/FEC remain an experimental
candidate until integration review. 1200 baud is the supported preferred next step; 600 baud is
a useful fallback candidate when noise margin matters more than packet rate.
